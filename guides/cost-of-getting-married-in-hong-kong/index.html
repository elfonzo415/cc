What does it cost to get married in Hong Kong?

Nikki Chui, solicitor and appointed Civil Celebrant of Marriages. Updated September 2026.

Short answer

The legal minimum is HK$1,020: a HK$305 notice fee plus HK$715 for a Marriage Registry ceremony on a weekday or Saturday morning. Marry at the registry on a weekend afternoon and the ceremony fee rises to HK$1,935. Marry in a licensed place of worship or through a civil celebrant and the government charges nothing for the ceremony; you pay the church or the celebrant instead.

Every price for a Hong Kong wedding you see quoted online is really two bills added together, and mixing them up is how couples get overcharged. One bill goes to the government and is fixed by law. The other goes to whoever runs your ceremony, and for a civil celebrant the law deliberately does not fix it. This guide separates the two, route by route, then covers the paper costs most couples only discover after the wedding. Every government figure below comes from the Immigration Department's own fee leaflet for marriage registration services. If you want the process itself, start with how to get married in Hong Kong.

What does the government charge, whichever route you take?

HK$305, once, for the Notice of Intended Marriage. Every couple pays it, whether the ceremony ends up at a registry, in a church, or on a rooftop with a celebrant.

The notice is filed on Form MR1(S), directly at a marriage registry or through a civil celebrant, and the Registrar exhibits it for at least 15 clear days before you can marry. The one way this fee doubles is timing: the marriage must happen within 3 months of the date notice was given. Let the notice expire and there is no extension and no refund. You file a fresh notice and pay a fresh HK$305, so the cheapest insurance in this whole guide is picking your date before you file.

How much is a Marriage Registry ceremony?

HK$715 during office hours, or HK$1,935 outside them. Office hours mean Monday to Friday and Saturday morning; the HK$1,935 rate covers Saturday afternoon and Sunday.

The fee buys a slot in one of the Immigration Department's own halls, the Registrar to officiate, and the marriage certificate signed on the day. It is the cheapest complete route, and the trade-off is the venue and the calendar: the ceremony happens in the registry's hall, in the registry's format, at whatever slots the registry has left. Popular dates go early, because registry bookings open up to 3 months ahead and auspicious dates are taken within days.

What does a church wedding cost?

The government charges nothing for the ceremony itself. A marriage in a licensed place of worship is solemnised by a competent minister of that church or body, and any fee is the church's own.

Most churches ask for a donation or a set charge that covers the minister, the building and sometimes the organist and flowers, and many marry only their own congregants or couples who complete their marriage preparation course. The HK$305 notice fee still applies, and the ceremony must run between 7am and 7pm with open doors.

How much does a civil celebrant cost?

Whatever you agree with the celebrant. The Immigration Department says it directly: "the law makes no provision regarding the fee charged by a civil celebrant."

That freedom is the point of the scheme, because a celebrant is the only route to a wedding outside a registry hall or a licensed place of worship: a hotel ballroom, a junk boat, your own living room, at any hour. But it also means quotes are not comparable until you know what each one includes. Some celebrants quote a bare officiating fee and add charges for filing the notice, travel, rehearsals or an out-of-hours ceremony. Ask every celebrant the same question: what is not included?

My own answer, so you have a benchmark: I charge HK$3,800 all inclusive. That covers filing the Notice of Intended Marriage with you, the ceremony anywhere in Hong Kong at any hour, in English, Cantonese or Mandarin, and the marriage certificate signed and in your hands on the day. The HK$305 government notice fee is the only thing on top, because it is the government's, not mine.

What is the cheapest way to get married in Hong Kong?

HK$1,020 all in: the HK$305 notice fee plus a HK$715 registry ceremony on a weekday or Saturday morning. That is a complete, legally registered marriage with the certificate included.

Nothing else is required by law. No rings, no photographer, no reception, no lawyer. If the budget matters more than the setting, take the weekday registry slot and put the difference toward the life after the wedding. If the setting matters and the registry calendar does not fit, that is what the celebrant route is for, and the comparison worth making is not HK$715 against a celebrant's fee, it is the whole package each route gives you for the money.

What paper costs come after the wedding?

You leave the ceremony with one signed marriage certificate. Extra proof of the marriage is where the later fees live, and they matter most if you will use the certificate abroad.

  • HK$280 for each certified copy of the marriage certificate from the Immigration Department.
  • HK$140 for a search of the marriage records, needed when the registry has to locate the entry first.
  • HK$125 per document for an apostille from the High Court, which is how a Hong Kong certificate is authenticated for use in another country.
  • A certified translation, priced by the translator, if the receiving authority does not accept English.

Visa applications, overseas property, foreign pension and insurance claims all tend to ask for a certified copy rather than your original, so couples who move abroad often order two or three copies at once and apostille them in the same trip.

What makes the bill grow when couples don't plan for it?

Three things, and none of them is a hidden government charge.

  • An expired notice. Marry more than 3 months after giving notice and you pay the HK$305 again, and possibly rebooking costs with your venue.
  • A quote that wasn't complete. A bare officiating fee can grow with add-ons for the notice filing, travel, an evening slot or a second language. Get the inclusions in writing before you compare prices.
  • The venue itself. No route's ceremony fee includes the room. A hotel or restaurant charges its own commercial rate whether the person officiating cost HK$715 or HK$7,000.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way to get married in Hong Kong?

HK$1,020 in total: the HK$305 notice fee plus HK$715 for a Marriage Registry ceremony on a weekday or Saturday morning. That is the full legal minimum, with the certificate included.

How much is the government fee to give notice of marriage?

HK$305, paid once when the Notice of Intended Marriage is filed. It covers every route. If the notice expires before you marry, you file a new notice and pay HK$305 again.

How much does a Marriage Registry ceremony cost?

HK$715 during office hours, Monday to Friday or Saturday morning, and HK$1,935 on a Saturday afternoon or Sunday. Both are on top of the HK$305 notice fee.

How much does a civil celebrant charge in Hong Kong?

The law does not fix a civil celebrant's fee; it is agreed privately. Ask any celebrant what their fee includes. I charge HK$3,800 all inclusive: filing the notice with you, the ceremony anywhere in Hong Kong, and the certificate signed on the day.

How much is a certified copy of a Hong Kong marriage certificate?

HK$280 per certified copy from the Immigration Department, plus HK$140 if a search of the marriage records is needed. An apostille from the High Court, for use abroad, is HK$125 per document.
